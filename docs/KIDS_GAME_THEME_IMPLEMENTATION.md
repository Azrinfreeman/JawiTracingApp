# A happier game world for Taman Jawi

Date: 2 October 2026  
Status: Option A selected and implementation authorised by the user; implemented and verified on 2 October 2026. See [verification](KIDS_GAME_THEME_VERIFICATION.md) and [assets](KIDS_GAME_THEME_ASSETS.md).

This document records the original four illustration concepts and implementation scope. The user subsequently chose A and authorised implementation and asset generation. Option A is now delivered; B, C and D remain comparison concepts. The original document-first boundary has been satisfied.

## The intended experience

Taman Jawi should feel like opening a colourful children's game: friendly characters greet the child, buttons look inviting to touch, letter choices belong to an illustrated world, and finishing a challenge feels like a small celebration.

The current theme already has a smiling sun, a leaf friend and garden artwork on the welcome screen. Much of the remaining interface uses white panels, thin borders and conventional cards. The proposed change carries a coherent illustrated world through the welcome screen, letter garden, mode selection, practice and trophy screens. Animation gives the characters personality and makes actions feel responsive.

**Recommendation: Option A, Taman Kawan Ceria.** It builds a storybook playground around the existing garden identity. Use rounded, chunky controls and sticker details to make the whole experience feel like a game.

Choose one main illustration direction for the release. These are alternatives to select from, rather than four additional themes children must configure.

### A — Taman Kawan Ceria: a storybook playground

**Imagine the opening illustration:** a smiling sun peeks over rounded green hills. A little leaf character waves beside an oversized flower-shaped play button. Flower and star friends carry balloons. A winding path leads to colourful letter stations, with a trophy pavilion in the distance. The scene has rounded shapes, expressive faces, a light paper texture and cheerful colour.

- **Palette:** warm cream, sunny yellow, grass green, sky blue and coral accents. Dark navy text stays easy to read.
- **Mascot:** evolve the existing leaf friend into a recognisable character with little arms, rosy cheeks and five poses: resting, waving, encouraging, celebrating and taking a break. Flower and star friends become consistent portraits for the existing profiles.
- **Letter selection:** large sticker-like letter stations sit along illustrated garden rows. Completed letters gain a flower badge. All currently available letters remain directly selectable; the path does not create new prerequisites.
- **Solo and Duo:** a garden challenge pavilion introduces the modes. Duo has equally sized player areas with different character badges and accent colours. Both boards have the same calm paper surface.
- **Trophy illustration:** a gold cup on a flower podium, with the companions cheering from the sides.
- **Animation ideas:** one welcome wave, occasional blinking, a brief flower-opening effect on completion and a short balloon celebration on results. Background movement stops during tracing.
- **Production consideration:** painted texture belongs in static scenery; animated characters should use simple shapes so they stay crisp and responsive on tablets.

**Illustration brief to produce after authorisation:** Original preschool storybook garden playground, rounded expressive leaf, flower and star companions, smiling sun, colourful hills, oversized flowers, balloons and stepping stones, warm cream background, soft painted texture, clean silhouettes and an open central area for interface controls. No text or alphabet characters inside the artwork; the application supplies all labels and Jawi glyphs.

### B — Dunia Mainan: a soft toy box

**Imagine the opening illustration:** a friendly rounded crayon waves from a colourful toy shelf. Soft building blocks, paper stars and puffy cushions frame a large toy-like play button. Letter choices look like substantial, rounded pieces a child could pick up. Shading is soft, with the charm of clay or plush toys.

- **Palette:** butter yellow, peach, mint, lavender and cream, with dark plum text.
- **Mascot:** an original smiling crayon with rounded hands and a soft clay finish. Flower, leaf and star profile portraits look like plush badges.
- **Letter selection:** chunky toy tiles arranged in clear rows on a toy shelf. The Jawi glyph remains flat, upright and unobstructed on each tile.
- **Solo and Duo:** one toy desk for Solo, two matching toy desks for Duo, each with a cushion-like player badge. The writing surfaces remain flat and equally sized.
- **Trophy illustration:** a rounded toy cup on colourful blocks, decorated with stars.
- **Animation ideas:** buttons depress on touch, the crayon gives a small nod, a completed-letter sticker pops into place, and the trophy rises briefly onto its podium.
- **Production consideration:** consistent clay shading takes more asset work. Keep the clay effect on scenery and control frames; avoid heavy shadows on the writing paper.

**Illustration brief to produce after authorisation:** Original cheerful preschool toy-box scene with a friendly rounded crayon character, soft clay blocks, plush flower, leaf and star badges, paper stickers and pastel cushions, gentle dimensional shading, warm even light, simple uncluttered shapes and space for controls. No text, letters, branded toys or existing entertainment characters.

### C — Hutan Sahabat: a friendly forest adventure

**Imagine the opening illustration:** a little kancil and rabbit greet the child at a sunny forest clearing. A bird holds a small banner above a path of leaf-shaped letter stations. Rounded trees, mushrooms, flowers and picnic flags make the scene feel like a children's picture book.

- **Palette:** fresh green, honey yellow, turquoise, soft orange and cream, with dark forest text.
- **Mascot:** an original kancil with a gentle expression; rabbit and bird companions support the scene. Existing profile names remain Bunga, Daun and Bintang, represented by illustrated badges.
- **Letter selection:** friendly signpost tiles in an easy-to-scan forest clearing. Decoration surrounds the labels rather than sharing their reading space.
- **Solo and Duo:** a practice clearing for Solo and two equal picnic tables for Duo, with matching scoreboard sizes and separate player badges.
- **Trophy illustration:** a woodland podium with a cup and cheering companions; leaf medals represent the existing result tiers.
- **Animation ideas:** a bird gives a brief wing wave on arrival, a mascot nods after completion, and leaf-shaped confetti appears on the result screen.
- **Production consideration:** multiple animals need consistent expressions and proportions. Use a small recurring cast and reserve richer scenes for welcome and results.

**Illustration brief to produce after authorisation:** Original preschool picture-book forest clearing with a friendly small kancil, rabbit and bird, rounded trees, flowers, picnic bunting, leaf badges and a sunlit path, soft hand-painted edges, bright welcoming colours, simple faces and an uncluttered centre. No written signs, alphabet glyphs or recognisable licensed characters.

### D — Angkasa Ceria: a space playground

**Imagine the opening illustration:** a small friendly robot waves beside a toy rocket on a pastel planet. Smiling stars, curved planet rings and colourful flags create an inviting adventure. A large launch-shaped play button starts the game, and letter choices appear as planet stations.

- **Palette:** light lavender, sky blue, peach, yellow and mint, with deep indigo text. Keep the overall background bright.
- **Mascot:** an original rounded robot with expressive eyes and a smiling star companion. Flower and leaf profile portraits gain playful space helmets alongside the existing star portrait.
- **Letter selection:** planet-shaped frames around flat, readable letter tiles. Keep the current catalogue order and direct access.
- **Solo and Duo:** a Solo explorer console and two equal Duo consoles. Player colours and portraits distinguish the sides; the writing boards stay light and clear.
- **Trophy illustration:** a star cup on a small planet podium, with a rocket parked beside it.
- **Animation ideas:** a single robot wave, a short rocket launch on entering the game and a brief star burst after a trophy is awarded. No moving star field during tracing.
- **Production consideration:** space props can add clutter quickly. Keep planets and rockets outside the writing area and use sparse background detail.

**Illustration brief to produce after authorisation:** Original cheerful preschool space playground, rounded friendly robot and smiling star, little toy rocket, pastel planets and simple flags, bright lavender and cream sky, clean cartoon shapes, soft shading and generous space for interface controls. No text, alphabet glyphs, dark cinematic atmosphere or licensed characters.

## How the selected theme changes the screens

| Screen | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Splash | A short character greeting around the existing academy branding; offer immediate entry without an animation delay. |
| Welcome | An illustrated game entrance, a prominent chunky “Jom main!” button, character portraits and large Solo/Duo choices. Simplify supporting copy. |
| Letter garden | Illustrated letter stations in accessible rows, clearly labelled names, flower/star completion badges and a playful progress display based on existing saved progress. |
| Practice | A themed frame around the stable writing paper, a small encouraging friend beside the guidance area, clear “Dengar”, “Cuba lagi” and navigation controls. |
| Challenge setup | Friendly game lobby with character badges and large round/time choices. Keep the existing rules easy for an adult to check. |
| Solo challenge | A compact game scoreboard and small character badge above the board; decoration stays outside the writing surface. |
| Duo 1v1 | Equal player spaces, cheerful player badges, a shared countdown and readable scores. Prioritise both boards and the pause control over decorative scenery. |
| Practice and match results | A short celebration, an illustrated podium where appropriate, clear result text and prominent replay/continue actions. A shared trophy gets an equally celebratory scene. |
| Teacher area | Carry through the palette and a small character accent while keeping review tools, tables, export and reset controls clear. |

Use chunky rounded buttons, visible pressed states, colourful section frames and illustrated badges throughout. Preserve normal keyboard focus, selected-state labels and touch targets. Keep Malay copy short and encouraging: “Jom main!”, “Sedia?”, “Hebat, siap!” and “Jom cuba lagi!”. Duo language describes both children playing together; it does not imply alternating turns.

Completion decoration reflects actual recorded progress. This proposal does not add currencies, purchases, streak penalties, new unlock rules or different match scoring.

## Animation plan

Movement should give actions personality while keeping handwriting comfortable and competition fair.

| Moment | Proposed motion | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome/selection | Character wave on arrival; infrequent blink; short scenery entrance | No mandatory wait before controls work; ambient motion settles within five seconds. |
| Button press | A small 100–160 ms depression or scale effect on the control surface | Keep the control's hit area fixed; no movement of adjacent layout. |
| Letter completion badge | A 250–400 ms sticker or flower pop | Trigger only for genuine completion; keep the label readable. |
| Challenge countdown | Brief emphasis on the existing 3, 2, 1 display | Use the existing countdown state; animations do not start or extend the clock. |
| Active tracing | Static scenery and resting mascot | Do not animate the board, its ancestors, guide positions or accepted ink; retain existing functional guidance. |
| Valid trace completed | One brief encouraging gesture outside the paper, up to 800 ms | Do not delay input acceptance, scoring or the next action. |
| Round result | A short badge/star burst after input has stopped | No overlay that catches touches or obscures scores. |
| Final trophy | A 1–1.5 second podium reveal and finite confetti burst | Replay and navigation remain available immediately. |
| Pause/recovery | Static resting mascot and clear pause message | Resume only through the existing shared pause flow. |

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: use static poses, immediate state changes and clear completion marks. Motion is never required to understand a result. Avoid flashes, screen shake, continuously bouncing buttons and repeated effects for every pointer movement. New animations are visual; existing approved letter-name recordings continue through the current audio controls.

## Delivery sequence

1. **Select the illustration direction.** Choose A, B, C or D. A combination such as A's playground with B's soft texture can be specified here, before assets are made.
2. **Authorise the work.** Produce the selected concept sheet and representative welcome, letter-selection and Duo compositions. Check the visual direction against the chosen brief, then use these references throughout integration.
3. **Build the shared theme and assets.** Establish the palette, characters, controls, frames and responsive art. Keep one coherent style across screens.
4. **Apply the theme to the existing flows.** Update welcome, garden, practice, challenge setup, Solo/Duo, results and teacher presentation.
5. **Add bounded animation.** Connect effects to existing states; include static reduced-motion equivalents and stop decoration during tracing.
6. **Verify and deliver.** Resolve relevant failures, record the tested build and captures, and update current theme/state documentation. Preserve previous verification records as history.

## Acceptance criteria

- The selected illustrated world is visible across the student journey, with recurring expressive characters and playful controls.
- Welcome, letter selection and trophy screens feel happy and recognisably game-like, including when motion is reduced.
- Children can readily identify what to press, what letter to trace and what happened after completing it.
- Artwork and animation remain outside a steady, legible tracing surface.
- Solo and Duo retain their existing rules, fair timing, equal playing space and saved results.
- Approved teaching content, recordings and the preschool tracing default remain intact.
- Relevant layout, input and build checks pass; remaining real-device limits are reported accurately.
